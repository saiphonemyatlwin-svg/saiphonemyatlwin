## Synthesis

### Consensus

1. **The fixed 15% tranche is a unanimous NO** — all five members, independently. It is an unsecured at-call promise from a shell company, behind senior debt that does not yet exist, payable from the same sponsor-controlled cash pool the principals privately discussed drawing on, with a 30-day withdrawal promise the structure cannot fund mid-project.
2. **The private post-call Hindi conversation ([71:31]–[74:08]) is the decisive evidence, and every member read it the same way.** Atul Saxena has quit his job and needs weekly personal income from this project; Ashish Malhotra's answer is "keep raising invoices, the payoff will keep happening" ("आप invoice raise करते रहना, payoff होता रहेगा"), with "we need ~250K each" ("हमें ढाई-ढाई लाख चाहिए") and a settled plan of a $25K lump sum now or ~$4K monthly, plus "I think we'll have to push them a bit" about getting investor money in. This was agreed minutes after telling investors that funds would not be used for anything else beyond "minor movements of 100 to 200 here and there, just temporarily." The invoicing rail is the very Vallum→Development-Six structure sold to investors on the call as *protection*.
3. **The corroborating on-call admissions are disqualifying on their own terms:** deliberately staying "under that AFSL radar"; renaming "development" to "investment" because lenders "don't like the word development" (misdescription of loan purpose); clearing site vegetation before the DA "so there's not going to be any objections"; and casual comfort with commingling investor funds.
4. **The financing story in the IM does not exist yet.** The modelled $11.1M senior facility is, in reality, one broker's *verbal* indication of ~$5.2M (80% of site value), no term sheet, valuation deliberately deferred, second broker not yet approached — and the sponsors privately agreed the loan application is now urgent.
5. **The cash position is thin and does not reconcile.** Of the "$1.5M committed": roughly $1.05–1.1M received, $450K still awaited, only ~$400K left in the accounts, ~$650K already spent pre-settlement — against a ~$3.5M equity requirement and a November settlement. Settlement failure is now a live left-tail scenario.
6. **The pricing cushion is gone.** The market has dropped from ~$880K to $835–840K per lot by the sponsors' own admission; the $825K model price is now ~1.5% below spot, not the ~6–8% buffer the IM implied. The 21-lot yield (R2/R5 split, MLS question) remains unresolved and carries ~15 points of RoC per lot.
7. **Sai's own exposure (flagged by Compliance):** on-distributing this offer to Singapore family offices could constitute unlicensed "arranging" of a financial product, and doing so with recorded knowledge of the private conversation risks accessorial liability. Do not on-refer this deal.

### Divergence

The only real split is whether the risks are *contractible*. The Deal Structurer and Market Analyst held that hard controls could in principle make the equity tranche investable: second-ranking registered mortgage + GSA with senior-lender intercreditor consent, two-signatory escrowed account with drawdowns only against independently certified invoices, a 10% preferred-return hurdle before the 20% promote, credit-approved debt terms, independent planning advice on the 21-lot yield, and written answers from both principals on how they are funding their living costs. The Devil's Advocate, Compliance Officer, and Sponsor Integrity Analyst held that no paper cures demonstrated intent — enforcement of any covenant would depend on the very people it is meant to constrain, across a 2.5–3.5-year illiquid hold. Notably, even the Deal Structurer expected the sponsors to refuse the drawdown-control condition — "their reaction to that single term is the cheapest diagnostic available" — and rated that refusal the correct resolution to NO-GO. The divergence is therefore about path, not destination.

### Recommendation

**Do not invest — either tranche, at any ticket size — and do not introduce the Singapore family-office capital.** The deal's economics were always secondary to sponsor and structure risk (the DD report said as much before the call); the transcript converts those risks from structural weaknesses into evidenced intent. If the relationship is worth preserving, the maximum defensible engagement is to present the Deal Structurer's condition set (escrowed two-signatory account, certified drawdowns, registered security with intercreditor consent, preferred-return hurdle, credit-approved term sheet, planning advice) as non-negotiable preconditions and let the sponsors' reaction be the final answer — with no funds moving under any circumstances until every condition is executed. The probability-weighted expectation of all five members is that this resolves to a walk-away.

## Risk assessment

From this lens, the deal fails before you reach the spreadsheet. The DD report (correctly) framed this as sponsor-risk, not market-risk, and scored structure 3/10 — but it assumed good-faith sponsors whose defects could be cured by conditions precedent. ## Risk assessment

From the compliance lens this is not a "fixable-with-conditions" deal; it is an issuer whose principals have stated, on tape, an intention to (a) mislead their senior financier about loan purpose, (b) extract undisclosed personal drawings from investor funds via raised invoices, (c) structure specifically to evade AFSL oversight while marketing to SMSFs that likely fail the wholesale test, and (d) pre-empt planning scrutiny by clearing vegetation. This is a plan to extract personal cash through the exact channel the structure was designed around — Vellum invoicing Auswide, presented on the call as investor protection ("no risk of this company being sued") — undisclosed to investors, and at ~$250K each it materially exceeds the disclosed $165K management fee schedule.

## Risk assessment

From a structuring lens the risks split three ways. **Contractible by hard control:** subordination (second-ranking registered mortgage + GSA/PPSR — verbally conceded, slot vacant), cash leakage (the invoice-extraction plan in finding 7 is defeated by physical control of the money, not by covenant: two-signatory account or solicitor escrow at Bensley, drawdowns only against independently certified invoices under an approved budget), waterfall ambiguity and the missing hurdle (drafting + the ~$2M leverage), capital-call/dilution mechanics, information and audit rights, key-person insurance as a condition precedent. **Contractible only as covenant/warranty — weaker:** related-party payment prohibitions, disclosure warranties from both principals, step-in rights; these depend on detection and on the economics of enforcing against two thin individuals from a $300K–$2M position, which are poor. **Not contractible at all:** senior-lender consent to junior security (unknowable until a credit-approved term sheet exists — finding 3 means the whole security package is conditional on a third party not yet in the room), the planning/MLS yield risk (DD §3.2, Risk 1), and sponsor integrity itself. Finding 7 does not merely add a risk — it demonstrates intent to defeat the disclosed fee structure, which means every covenant-grade protection must be discounted to near zero and only hard controls counted. Crucially, the structure test is also the integrity test: sponsors who genuinely need weekly personal cash cannot accept escrowed, certified drawdowns — their reaction to that single term is the cheapest diagnostic available.

## Verdict

**CONDITIONAL-GO — equity tranche only, and only if every condition below is executed before a dollar moves; the fixed tranche is a NO under any terms; failure or dilution of any condition = walk.** The subordination, waterfall, fee and leakage risks are all technically contractible, and Sai holds unusual leverage (~$2M unraised, sponsors' self-imposed August–September deadline, an on-record "whatever security you want" concession).

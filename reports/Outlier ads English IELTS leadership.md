# Fix One Mistake, Then Sell the Fix

Across all three categories, the ads that outperformed did one thing in the first three seconds: they named a specific mistake or score and then showed it being fixed. English apps did this with "coach corrects your sentence" and "never say 'I'm fine, thank you'" demos. IELTS sellers did it with band-score reveals and a free placement test. Leadership sellers did it with podcast-style clips that open on a data point or a contrarian line. The best-documented single outlier is Praktika's creator video of a coach correcting a learner in a short conversation. It drew about **30M organic views across Instagram, TikTok and YouTube**, and the CEO says it took the company from **about $1M to $10–12M ARR in one month** ([Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-praktika-built-a-profitable-50m-arr-ai-business-adam-turaev-praktika)). That figure is a founder claim, not an audited one. Most other "winners" can only be identified through proxies: Meta Ad Library run length and variant counts, TikTok Creative Center percentile rankings, and YouTube view ratios. None of them proves profit. Paid winners are usually organic winners re-shot at scale, and "guarantee" or income-claim hooks are both the most common and the most policy-exposed. For a Burmese-speaking seller, the data points to a clear operating model. TikTok now reaches **32.2M Myanmar adults against Facebook's 11.1M** ([DataReportal 2027](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2027-myanmar)), so TikTok is the place to win attention. Messenger and KBZPay/WavePay are where buyers close ([Lwin Agency](https://www.lwinagency.com/en/blog/why-facebook-marketing-is-important-for-business-in-myanmar)). The creative should use mistake-correction hooks, honest base-to-result testimonials, a free diagnostic test as the entry offer, and framing built on careers and opportunity that stays clear of politics.

## English apps win by fixing one sentence on camera

The format behind the category's breakout is now industry standard. Praktika's CEO says the Gustavo video "is now the main structure of the video for language learning apps these days". After it went viral, the company replicated it "with other creators in other countries" using micro-creators with **under 5,000 followers**, paid a fixed fee plus a view bonus ([Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-praktika-built-a-profitable-50m-arr-ai-business-adam-turaev-praktika)). The paid team did not invent the winner; it inherited it from organic. The ads still visible in Praktika's Meta library are small and ordinary: **22 ads, median run 48 days** ([Sprites](https://www.sprites.ai/praktika-ai-ads)). Anyone studying only the ad library would miss the real outlier.

**Hooks.** The dominant 2025–2026 hook is the **myth correction**: "Never, and I mean never, say 'How are you?'" ([Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1516510946393831)), Loora's "Never, and I mean never, say these: 'I'm fine. Thank you.'" ([Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1044302045184453)), and Falou's "NEVER say these 5 words in Japan (wait for the last one..." ([Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1478973174137494)). Learna runs the same line in Spanish, German, Ukrainian, Turkish, Chinese, Thai and Hindi. The surrounding sentence is in the viewer's language and only the English phrase stays English, as in the Thai "เลิกพูดว่า I'm fine, thank you" ([Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=881817237987628)). That localization trick transfers directly to Burmese. The second hook family is **"I understand but I can't speak"** (Praktika, Loora), which names the most common private frustration of exam-trained learners. The third is **career fear**: ELSA's longest-running Vietnamese ad starts from the message "Can you join the meeting?" ([Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/app-revenue/elsa-speak)), and Preply's job-interview ad asks "What do I say if they ask about my strengths?" ([Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=962161846879306)). The fourth is **family shame**, as in Learna's Hindi "the daughter was ashamed of her mother's English" ([Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1017250967460654)). That works in South Asia but is risky in a face-saving culture, as discussed below. The last family is **entertainment quizzes**: Preply's "Did you guess it right 🤯" creator videos have run 160–388 days ([AdManage](https://admanage.ai/ad-library/preply)).

**Why the myth-correction hook outperformed.** It packs four mechanics into five seconds. It threatens mild embarrassment over a phrase every textbook learner knows. It pays off instantly, because the viewer learns something before deciding to scroll. It bridges naturally to the product, since the tutor corrects you. And it localizes cheaply. The AI or human tutor on screen turns the hook's claim into a live demonstration, so the proof sits inside the ad.

**Angles and markets.** Asian and emerging-market ads lean on **career, income and family**. Praktika's Brazil team put it bluntly: "We didn't sell English to them. We sold them an opportunity to get a better job" ([Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-praktika-built-a-profitable-50m-arr-ai-business-adam-turaev-praktika)). US and European ads lean on **speed and method**: Babbel's "3 weeks", and Speak's "3.7× faster" and "1,000+ Spanish sentences in a week" ([AdManage](https://admanage.ai/ad-library/speak)). Myanmar sits firmly in the first group.

**Offers and CTAs.** Offers cluster into four types. The first is free trials (Speak's "Try Speak free for 7 days", ELSA's 7-day Premium). The second is deep anchored discounts (ELSA's "999K (old price 2,745K)", Babbel's "50% off Lifetime"). The third is creator codes ("Use the link in my BIO and get 50% OFF on your first lesson"). The fourth is price-vs-tutor anchors, such as the "50X cheaper than a tutor" claim Praktika says it originated in Brazil ([Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-praktika-built-a-profitable-50m-arr-ai-business-adam-turaev-praktika)). ELSA also ran click-to-Messenger ads with an AI agent qualifying leads in markets without a local sales team, reporting more leads and higher order values but no figures ([Meta for Business](https://www.facebook.com/business/success/elsa-speak-bm-success-story)). That is the closest documented precedent for a Messenger-first English funnel.

**Length and format.** Short brand-style TikTok ads win at **10–17 seconds** (Busuu). AI-tutor demos run **54–96 seconds** because the demo itself is the content (Learna) ([TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/inspiration/topads/)). On Meta, static images still carry offers: **79% of Babbel's active ads are images** ([AdManage](https://admanage.ai/ad-library/babbel)), and ELSA's top Vietnamese ad was a static image. Variant counts in the thousands (Speak's 2,630, Preply's 1,643 for "Speak English Confidently") show that a "winning ad" is really a winning copy-plus-format template fed to dynamic creative.

One caution on edgy content comes from Praktika itself. "Unhinged avatars were going viral in upper funnel", but bringing that tone into the product upset users, because "we're not serving AI companions, we're serving AI coaches" ([Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-praktika-built-a-profitable-50m-arr-ai-business-adam-turaev-praktika)). Reach-driving content still has to match the promise of the product.

## IELTS outliers sell a band number, a free test and visible proof

IELTS has two winning formats, and they are almost opposites.

**The organic outlier is the band-score reveal.** A real candidate sits a mock speaking test on camera and the band appears in the title. Ross IELTS Academy's Band 9 test has **11.59M views**, and its **Band 2.5** test has **6.08M**, more than half the Band 9 video's audience ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7vzC-qMb0jQ)). Both ends of the scale work. Band 9 sells aspiration ("what does perfect sound like?") and Band 2.5 sells curiosity. IELTS Advantage has turned the format into a factory. It republishes "Perfect Band 9" tests with new candidates every few months, each still reaching **280K–565K views**, and it holds **13 of the top 20** view-ranked IELTS slots for the past year. Its titles carry a "[+ Free Speaking PDF]" lead magnet, so each video doubles as an email opt-in ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k4715CJ0Ii8)). South Asia adds two local twists: the **celebrity mock test** (Munzereen Shahid with actress Tasnia Farin, 1.46M views) and **view-count social proof in titles** (Asad Yaqub's "2.3 MILLION WATCHED!"). Western creators largely ignore both.

**The paid outlier is the stacked offer.** The top TikTok IELTS ad of the last 180 days, a Vietnamese 35-second lead-gen ad with **124,919 likes, about 7× the next ad**, packs four levers into its on-screen first line: low time cost ("30 minutes a day"), 1-on-1 tutoring, a guaranteed band ("7.0+"), and "50% off plus free test" ([TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/inspiration/topads/)). The #3 and #4 ads repeat the structure, and about **11 of the top 20** IELTS ads are Vietnamese lead-gen. On Meta, the evergreen Vietnamese winners look nothing like ads. Study4 runs **native-looking "real user review" posts from personal self-study pages**. They open with the reverse-psychology line "❌❌ Don't waste money signing up for Study4's 6.5+ course, guys 👇😂" and then give a specific base-to-result story: "spent nearly 2 million… scored 6.5 after 6 months (base 5.0)". These ads have run for **17–21 months** ([Meta Ad Library VN](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?q=IELTS&country=VN)). DOL English shows a third proof style: named students from named elite schools, IELTS scores paired with SAT scores, and a branded method ("Linearthinking") in nearly every caption.

**Funnels split cleanly by region.** Asian centres run **"Send message" funnels**: Messenger in Vietnam and Myanmar, WhatsApp across South Asia. They are built on a free placement test, free mock test or free demo class. Western creators run **email funnels** (free PDF, free 5-day challenge) that end in a VIP course with no sales call. The official bodies run **"book the test, get US$170 of prep free"** ([Meta Ad Library](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?q=IELTS)). The free test is the most common entry offer in Asia because it is also a sales diagnostic: "you're 5.5, you need 7.0, here's the plan". The one quantified Indian case found about **0.9% lead-to-enrolment** from Google leads (2,864 leads, 26 enrolments, agency self-reported) ([Muneez Ahmed](https://www.muneezahmed.com/case-studies/ielts-google)). That rate helps explain why conversational chat funnels beat form fills.

**The policy tension is real.** "Cam kết đầu ra" ("guaranteed output score") is the standard Vietnamese hook and appears among the top-performing TikTok ads. TikTok's written policy, however, says ad content "must not promise or exaggerate results" ([TikTok Ads Policy](https://ads.tiktok.com/resources/help/article/tiktok-ads-policy-misleading-and-false-content)). Under Meta's misleading-claims rules, outcome testimonials should be marked as non-typical and set against typical outcomes ([AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-ad-misleading-claims-personal-attributes-prohibited-content-policy-2026)). Guarantees also create reputational risk. IELTS Fighter had to publish a rebuttal post titled "the truth about IELTS Fighter's output guarantee: rumour or fact?" ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/ieltsfighter/posts/-th%E1%BB%B1c-h%C6%B0-chuy%E1%BB%87n-ielts-fighter-cam-k%E1%BA%BFt-%C4%91%E1%BA%A7u-ra-l%E1%BB%9Di-%C4%91%E1%BB%93n-hay-s%E1%BB%B1-th%E1%BA%ADt-d%E1%BA%A1o-g%E1%BA%A7n-%C4%91%C3%A2y-tr%C3%AA/1142618498135537/)). British Council and IDP never guarantee scores; they compete on verifiable logistics such as two-day results, One Skill Retake and free date changes. The guarantee works, but it is borrowed against future rejections and backlash. The Study4 pattern of "base 5.0 → 6.5 in 6 months, real user" delivers the same specificity without the promise, and it has the longest run time in the sample.

## Leadership infomercials moved from Lamborghini pre-rolls to podcast clips

The classic 2015–2020 long-form pre-roll worked for reasons that no longer hold. Tai Lopez's "Here in my garage" opened on a Lamborghini because the first five seconds were designed around the skip button. It then pivoted to "knowledge, not cars", told a rags-to-riches origin story, deliberately polarized viewers ("I don't want cynics"), and closed on a free, low-friction CTA ("three things I learned from my mentors") that fed a **$67-a-month** back end ([Systeme.io](https://systeme.io/blog/here-in-my-garage); [Leon Shi](https://leonlshi.medium.com/how-tai-lopez-became-famous-a-deep-dive-into-his-viral-video-9a3dbc6952e2)). Its reach claims are Lopez's own: VICE noted they were paid views ([VICE](https://www.vice.com/en/article/inside-the-garage-of-the-internets-most-hated-self-help-guru-511/)). The same frequency that built these brands turned them into memes and "scam" reaction content. Lopez now faces SEC charges over an alleged ~$112M Ponzi scheme in a separate retail venture ([The Drive](https://www.thedrive.com/news/sec-says-here-in-my-garage-youtuber-tai-lopez-ran-a-ponzi-scheme)). In India, Vivek Bindra's Bada Business was publicly accused of clickbait quick-wealth ads after Sandeep Maheshwari's December 2023 exposé video ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/vivek-bindra-sold-ambani-zuckerberg-dreams-to-indias-lost-youth-its-now-a-nightmare/1902941); [TechStory](https://techstory.in/decoding-the-sandeep-maheshwari-and-vivek-bindra-controversy/)). Income-claim creative now carries regulatory and reputational liability as well as policy risk.

The surviving model is Hormozi's: **content as ads**. Each piece is a "content unit" built as hook, retain, reward ([Deven Davis](https://www.devendavis.com/book-summaries/100m-leads)). Organic clips that win are "run as ads, adding an overlay" ([Sozai transcript](https://sozai.app/transcript/alex-hormozi-facebook-ads-strategy-2026/)). Hooks are the main variable. Decoded examples include a **68-second "data point start"** and a **40-second "role-specific opening"**, both cut from podcast interviews ([Heista](https://www.heista.co/decode/alex-hormozi-interview-podcast-data-point-start)). His 2025 playbook lists five ad types: demonstration, testimonial, education, story and faceless. Within testimonial it names "podcast style, founder direct to camera, walk-n-talk rant" ([GitHub copy of $100M Playbook](https://github.com/jabjab42/hormozi-books/blob/main/$100M%20Playbook_%20GOATed%20Ads%20--%20Alex%20Hormozi%20--%202025.md)). The front end is self-liquidating. Reported pre-launch ad spend of about $4.5M returned $6.5M before the $100M Money Models launch began, and the book sold about **2.9M copies in a day** ([Solopreneur Code](https://solopreneurcode.substack.com/p/alex-hormozi-100m-money-models-book-launch); [SelfPublishing.com](https://selfpublishing.com/alex-hormozi-book-launch/)). The revenue figures differ between secondary sources. MasterClass shows the opposite emotional route: **aspiration and borrowed authority** instead of income. It uses cinematic 15–60-second trailers and one variant per instructor and interest ([AdLibrary](https://adlibrary.com/brands/masterclass); [Digiday](https://digiday.com/media/with-much-of-the-world-at-home-masterclass-seizes-its-moment)).

Funnels after leadership ads follow four repeatable shapes. The first is a free video to a low-ticket subscription (Lopez). The second is a free webinar or livestream to a $2k–5k program: Robbins and Graziosi claimed **250,000 attendees** for one live event ([EOFire](https://www.eofire.com/lessons-learned-from-our-biggest-affiliate-launch/)). The third is a free-plus-shipping book to upsells (Brunson, Hormozi). The fourth is an application to a sales call to a $5k–10k program, such as Dan Lok's **$6,997** High Ticket Closer ([ippei.com](https://ippei.com/high-ticket-closer-3-0/)). Affiliate leaderboards were the multiplier in the KBB and Hormozi launches. In Myanmar, sourced evidence shows the local formats rather than their performance. These include "Morning Motivation" monologues and multi-day seminars with "Millionaire" framing (Lynn Thaik Nyunt), "No.1 CEO Coach" self-positioning (Naing Win Aung), coach certifications (Breakthrough), and one-word podcast clips (It's Mint) ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/ltnmyanmarland/videos/morning-motivation/1624765799039868/); [Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/naingwinaungexecutivecoach/)). No ad-level data exists for Southeast Asian leadership coaches, so local copying has to be validated in the ad libraries.

## Platforms reward different lengths, proof and openings

The platform differences come down to three variables: how long viewers will stay, what counts as proof, and where the sale closes.

| Dimension | TikTok | Meta (FB/IG Reels, Feed) | YouTube |
|---|---|---|---|
| Winning length in this research | 10–17s for brand/convenience (Busuu); 16–71s for IELTS lead gen; 54–96s for AI-tutor demos | 15–60s video; static images still carry offers (Babbel 79% image; ELSA's 71-day winner was static) | 10–70 min organic (Ross 17:24, IELTS Advantage 1:08:54); 40–68s podcast clips as ads |

Two findings matter most for allocation. First, education ads have a **hook problem, not a form problem**. In WordStream's 2026 US benchmarks, Education & Instruction lead ads convert at **15.87%** against an all-industry 8.54%, yet their CTR is among the lowest of any industry at **1.74%** ([WordStream 2026](https://www.wordstream.com/blog/facebook-ads-benchmarks-2026)). Creative effort should go to the first three seconds. Second, the **"multiple versions" label on Meta is noisy**. It also appears when Dynamic Creative or placement customization is switched on, so it does not always mean deliberate iteration on a winner ([Sprout Social](https://support.sproutsocial.com/hc/en-us/articles/360050699272-How-do-I-identify-ads-with-multiple-versions)).

## Finding outliers yourself takes three ratios and a weekly routine

No platform tells outsiders whether a competitor's ad makes money, so the method is to combine proxies and then confirm with your own numbers.

**Meta Ad Library (longevity × duplication).** Search the competitor's Page, not a keyword, and keep the default "Impressions: high to low" sort. Record three signals: the start date (still active and 30+ days old is the first filter; 90+ is strong), the "X ads use this creative and text" count, and the rank in the sort ([Succession Media](https://succession.media/guides/meta-ads-library/); [Eden](https://eden.so/blog/facebook-ad-library/)). A workable score is **days active × number of ads sharing the creative**. Give extra weight to the same creative appearing under several separate ad IDs, and discount "multiple versions" that come only from DCO format variants. For Burmese-speaking audiences, search country = Myanmar, Thailand, Malaysia, Singapore and Japan, using the keywords "IELTS", "English Speaking", "အင်္ဂလိပ်စာ", "Leadership" and "ခေါင်းဆောင်". Also search the Pages of the official bodies (British Council Myanmar, Take IELTS Official) and the AI apps already targeting Myanmar (Stimuler, Magoosh). In Vietnam, also check personal and "self-study" pages, because Study4's evergreen ads run through them.

**TikTok Creative Center (percentile plus retention curve).** Open Top Ads and filter by region, Education industry, and the lead-generation or conversion objective. Sort by CTR and read the percentile badges ("Top 10% CTR"). Then open each ad's **interactive time analysis**, which plots CTR, CVR and retention second by second, to see exactly where viewers drop or convert ([TikTok Creative Center](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/inspiration/topads/pc/en); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/tiktok-creative-center/)). Use Keyword Insights to test which phrases ("IELTS", "speaking", "free test") carry above-average CTR. Myanmar may not be a selectable region. Use Vietnam, Thailand and Indonesia as proxies, and search TikTok itself in Burmese script for organic outliers.

**YouTube (views ÷ channel median).** Divide a video's views by the median of the channel's last 10–20 uploads. **3× is worth a look; 5× is worth studying**. Keep Shorts and long-form baselines separate, and include small channels, since a 3× on a small channel teaches as much as a 10× on a large one ([1of10](https://1of10.com/blog/viral-youtube-video-ideas-outlier-method/); [OutlierKit](https://outlierkit.com/resources/outlier-scores/)). Search "IELTS speaking band", "English speaking Myanmar" and Burmese-script queries sorted by view count, both all-time and this year.

**Your own ads (internal truth beats proxies).** Triage in this order: thumbstop (3-second views ÷ impressions), then hold rate, then CTR, then cost per Messenger conversation or lead against your account median. A vendor's bands put **under 20% thumbstop as weak, 30%+ as strong and 45%+ as exceptional**. They warn that a 45% thumbstop with a 10% hold means "you baited the viewer and delivered nothing" ([Popjam](https://popjam.io/blog/thumbstop-rate)). For Myanmar, set baselines from your first 2–4 weeks. No Myanmar CPM or CPL data exists. Southeast Asian Facebook CPMs run roughly **$1–3.40** ([Stackmatix](https://www.stackmatix.com/blog/facebook-ads-cpm-by-country-2026); [Lebesgue](https://lebesgue.io/facebook-ads/facebook-cpm-by-country)), so US education CPLs of about $26 are irrelevant.

**Weekly routine.** On Monday, pull the 3 oldest active ads for 5 competitors and the top 10 TikTok Top Ads in Education for your proxy regions. Log each in a swipe file with these columns: hook text (first 3 seconds), format, angle, offer, CTA, length, signal and evidence grade. On Wednesday, re-shoot the strongest 2 structures in Burmese with your own teacher. By Friday, kill anything under 20% thumbstop and move organic posts that beat 3× your median into Spark Ads or boosted posts. This is Praktika's loop at small scale: detect organically, then pay to scale.

## Burmese playbook: TikTok for reach, Messenger to close, careers as the promise

**Channel mix.** TikTok's Myanmar adult ad reach grew **53% in a year to 32.2M**, while Facebook's reported reach fell **19% to 11.1M** ([DataReportal 2027](https://datareportal.com/reports/digital-2027-myanmar)). Facebook has been blocked since February 2021 and needs a VPN, and VPN use was further restricted in 2024–25 ([Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/graphics/MYANMAR-POLITICS/INTERNET-RESTRICTION/rlgpdbreepo/); [Human Rights Myanmar](https://humanrightsmyanmar.org/the-great-firewall-of-myanmar/)). TikTok should therefore be the attention engine and creative test bed. Facebook and Messenger remain the trust layer and the checkout: buyers message the Page, the sales team confirms, and payment arrives "usually via mobile wallets like KBZPay, Wave Pay, or cash on delivery" ([Lwin Agency](https://www.lwinagency.com/en/blog/why-facebook-marketing-is-important-for-business-in-myanmar)). Meta ads for Myanmar can only be paid by international card or PayPal ([Meta Business Help](https://www.facebook.com/business/help/1894619360792030)), so budget for a diaspora card, an agency or a partner. Whether Myanmar-based advertisers can open TikTok ad accounts was not verified. Organic TikTok plus boosting through a diaspora-registered account is the fallback. Target by **Burmese language across MM, TH, MY, SG and JP**, not by Myanmar geography alone. Diaspora users are counted in their host countries, and Japan's Myanmar population rose **126% between 2023 and 2025** ([Lowy Institute](https://www.lowyinstitute.org/the-interpreter/japan-needs-to-separate-protection-from-migration-for-myanmar)). Use YouTube for organic long-form trust content (mock tests, full lessons, workshop replays), because no Myanmar YouTube reach figure exists to justify paid spend there.

**Funnel.** TikTok or Reels video → comment or DM keyword ("TEST", "MOCK", "LEAD") or click-to-Messenger → **free diagnostic** (English speaking level check, IELTS mini-mock with estimated band, or a leadership-style self-assessment) → 10-minute chat or voice consult that maps current level to target → low-ticket entry (paid 3-day challenge or workshop, so cold traffic pays back as in Hormozi's self-liquidating model) → main course paid by KBZPay/WavePay screenshot in Myanmar or by local transfer in THB/JPY/SGD for the diaspora → onboarding into a Telegram or Messenger group. The free test does double duty, as it does for Asian IELTS centres: it is the lead magnet and the sales script. For IELTS buyers, address the fee friction directly. The British Council Myanmar exam costs **USD 190–235**, and since 20 November 2025 it can only be paid online by international card ([British Council Myanmar](https://www.britishcouncil.org.mm/exam/ielts/dates-fees-locations)). "Don't pay the exam fee twice" is a true, specific and powerful frame, and a short "how to book and pay" guide is a useful lead magnet in its own right.

**Creative system.** Feed each ad set **5–10 genuinely different concepts**, as Andromeda rewards ([Dentsu](https://www.dentsu.com/ae/en/our-latest-thinking/meta-andromeda-strategy)), and run 3–5 per TikTok ad group. The concepts are: a mistake-correction micro-lesson, a live coach-corrects-student role-play, a band-reveal sample comparison, an honest student base-to-result journey, a stacked-offer card, a workplace scenario for the diaspora, and for leadership a podcast clip or boss-vs-leader contrast. Follow TikTok's specs: proposition in the first 3 seconds, hook by 6, 9:16, sound on, **Burmese captions on every line** (viewers on mute or slow VPN connections must still follow), and light files. Keep the teacher's face visible. Use ဆရာ / ဆရာမ honorifics and an elder-mentor tone rather than brash guru hype. The local "No.1" self-titles do exist, but they borrow trust the brand would be wiser to earn with proof.

**Claims that stay policy-safe.** Replace "guaranteed Band 7" with "a target-band plan" plus a written free re-study policy with terms on the chat or landing page. Replace aggregate boasts with named, consenting students giving **base score, timeframe and actual result**, plus the line "ရလဒ်များ လူတစ်ဦးချင်းအလိုက် ကွာခြားနိုင်ပါသည်" ("results vary by individual"). Google requires a visible disclaimer next to result testimonials, and it is good practice on Meta and TikTok ([Tagada](https://www.tagada.io/payments/info-products/advertising)). Avoid second-person attribute hooks such as "Is your English bad?" and use first or third person instead ("Many learners make this mistake"; "I understand English but can't speak"). Meta's personal-attributes rule turns on exactly that "you + attribute" pattern ([AuditSocials](https://www.auditsocials.com/blog/meta-ad-misleading-claims-personal-attributes-prohibited-content-policy-2026)). For leadership, make **no income claims and show no cash, cars or luxury**. TikTok bans "flaunting or showing off money" and all three platforms ban unrealistic financial-reward claims ([Tagada](https://www.tagada.io/payments/info-products/advertising)). Frame outcomes as skills, team results and career progress. Avoid public shaming of poor English, including the "Band 2.5 reveal" of a real person and family-shame scripts. Saving face matters, and mocking a real candidate in paid media also invites policy review. Use teacher-performed sample answers instead.

**Politically neutral framing.** The demand is real and partly driven by politics. The conscription law activated in February 2024 triggered "hasty exit strategies" among young people ([CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2024/02/22/asia/myanmar-mandatory-conscription-fears-young-people-intl-hnk-dst)), and about 2.3M Myanmar migrants were registered in Thailand by mid-2024 ([East Asia Forum](https://eastasiaforum.org/2024/10/26/myanmar-faces-manifold-crises-as-military-conscription-drives-mass-exodus/)). Ads should never say so. Keep every line inside **"future, career, study abroad, international work, confidence"**. Never mention conscription, the military, resistance groups, elections, sanctions or "escape". Avoid flags, protest imagery, news footage and location references to conflict areas, and keep comment moderation neutral too. This protects the advertiser, the buyers and the account.

**Offer ladder and pricing.** The research found no sourced MMK course prices. Set prices from your own tests, keeping in mind that poverty nearly doubled to almost half the population by 2023 ([East Asia Forum](https://eastasiaforum.org/2024/10/26/myanmar-faces-manifold-crises-as-military-conscription-drives-mass-exodus/)). That argues for a free diagnostic, a low-ticket paid entry, installments, and separate diaspora price cards in THB, JPY and SGD, with workplace English for Thailand and professional and leadership content for Japan and Singapore.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which ad should we copy?" to "which loop should we run?". The documented breakouts did not come from clever paid creative. They came from organic formats found by volume and then re-shot at scale: Praktika's creator correction, Duolingo's character, IELTS Advantage's repeating band-reveal factory, and Hormozi's clip library. For a Burmese-speaking brand this is good news. The winning structures cost little to make with one credible teacher, and TikTok's audience in Myanmar is now about three times Facebook's reported reach, so organic detection is cheap before any money is spent.

The second implication is that honesty is a competitive advantage here, not only a compliance cost. The longest-running IELTS ads in the sample promise nothing; they show a real person moving from 5.0 to 6.5 over six months. The guarantee and income-claim creative that dominates Vietnam and the old guru era carries rejection risk, backlash and, in the worst cases, regulators. A brand selling hope to a price-sensitive, mobile-first and politically pressured audience will last longer by doing what the evidence suggests: fix one real mistake on camera, offer a free diagnostic, close warmly in Messenger, and keep every promise about the learner's future rather than the country's politics.

## 1. Executive Summary & IC View

The deal's underlying economics are plausible and the headline pricing assumption ($825k per R2 lot vs $875k–$897k comparable evidence) is genuinely conservative. The IM's arithmetic is internally consistent — our rebuilt model reproduces every headline figure to the dollar. The location thesis checks out: the Caledonia Precinct was rezoned in February 2019 for approximately 170 dwellings, and the adjoining Caledonia Estate (Ankira Projects, 14 lots of 500–2,000 sqm) is actively selling with land registration dated March 2026 — precinct momentum is real, not marketing.

However, this is a **sponsor-risk and structure-risk deal, not a market-risk deal**. The material issues are: (1) investors are structurally unsecured behind an $11.1M senior facility, holding shares in a shell company that has only a contractual claim on the project entity; (2) the sponsor group is thin — two-person principals, SPVs with no trading history, and a track record that is self-reported and unverifiable from public sources; (3) a related-party acquisition fee flows to a principal's own company without related-party disclosure; (4) the minimum lot size across the site is unconfirmed, which means the 21-lot yield — the single largest value driver — may require more than a standard DA; and (5) the offer's compliance posture (s708 wholesale offer that simultaneously references "retail" investor documentation) is internally contradictory and warrants legal review.

**IC-style verdict: Conditional — proceed to confirmatory DD only.** The return profile compensates for market risk but is not yet demonstrated to compensate for structural subordination and sponsor concentration. The conditions precedent in Section 8 should be satisfied before any capital commitment. On the two tranches: the fixed tranche prices like mezzanine risk paid a bank-deposit-plus return (~13.6% IRR for an unsecured position); the shareholding tranche carries the real risk but is the only tranche whose return (~24–31% IRR modelled) is commensurate with where investors actually sit in the capital stack.

### 2.4 Compliance observations

- The IM states it is a s708 wholesale-only offer not lodged with ASIC, yet Section 7.3 references "separate documentation for retail and super (SMSF) investors." A wholesale exemption offer that contemplates retail participants is internally contradictory; many SMSF trustees do not meet the wholesale test. If the offer is in fact being distributed to non-wholesale investors, the issuer has a Corporations Act problem — which becomes an investor problem (regulatory intervention, unwind risk, co-investor quality).
- A "fixed-return share" with a 30-day withdrawal mechanism functions economically like an at-call debt instrument issued by a shell company. How withdrawals are funded mid-project (when all cash is in land and civils) is unexplained — this is a liquidity promise the structure cannot obviously honour.

## 3. Site & Market Verification

### 3.1 Verified

- **Site:** 1.818 ha at the end of a cul-de-sac; listed for sale via Harcourts The Property People around August 2025 as a Caledonia Precinct growth-area opportunity. Size and positioning match the IM.
- **Precinct:** The Caledonia Precinct (bounded by Mercedes, Bensley and Oxford Roads) was rezoned under Campbelltown LEP 2015 Amendment 13 (gateway December 2016; rezoned February 2019) to permit approximately 170 dwellings, with a Voluntary Planning Agreement delivering ~$2.13M of infrastructure. The precinct thesis in the IM is genuine.
- **Adjoining demand:** Caledonia Estate (Ankira Projects, 308 Bensley Road) — 14 lots of 500–2,000 sqm including duplex sites, actively marketed with land registration dated 26 March 2026. The IM's "$897k off-the-plan lot next door" is consistent with this estate's product.
- **Constraints:** Bushfire risk is detected on planning overlays for the site — consistent with the IM's flag and the precinct's bushfire constraints reports. APZ (asset protection zone) requirements could consume developable area.

### 3.2 Issues found

- **The $1.351M "comparable" at 5 Daimler Place is a 4-bedroom house, not a vacant lot.** Street sales data shows 5 Daimler as a 4-bed/2-bath dwelling. Using it as pricing evidence for subdivision lots is misleading; it is at best evidence of end-value in the street. The genuinely comparable evidence is one lot sale ($875k, 11 Claydon Rd) and one off-the-plan next door ($897k) — thin, though directionally supportive of $825k.
- **Minimum lot size is the buried risk.** The IM states MLS is "variable across the site — to be confirmed through the DA." MLS is set by the LEP, not by a DA. Nearby land (26 Mercedes Road) required a full LEP amendment to reduce MLS from 2 ha to 500 sqm before small-lot subdivision was possible. If any material part of 9 Daimler Place carries a large MLS or Environmental Living-legacy controls, achieving 21 lots requires a **planning proposal (rezoning/LEP amendment)** — a 12–24 month process with Council and Gateway discretion — not a 12-month DA. This single question can move the timeline, the yield, or both.
- **$2.7M (15%) of GRV sits in two unevidenced lots** — the $1.5M "larger/commercial (C5)" lot and the $1.2M existing-dwelling lot. No comparable is offered for either. Note C5 under the standard NSW instrument is _Environmental — coastal/scenic protection_-type zoning in some LEPs; what "C5" means here, and whether a commercial use is even permissible, is unexplained.
- **Entry price:** $6.5M for 18,166 sqm = ~$358/sqm englobo, ~35% of GRV. Within normal range for zoned subdivision land, but the site was on market from ~August 2025 and settled terms are not disclosed; the vendor's consent-to-lodge suggests a delayed settlement (~Nov 2026), which is sponsor-favourable.

## 4. Feasibility Model — Verification & Stress Testing

Our rebuilt model reproduces the IM exactly: GRV $18,375,000; total cost $14,671,771; profit $3,703,229; senior facility $11,107,688; equity $3,564,084; net to investors $2,580,519; RoC 72.4%. Cost assumptions ($175k/lot civils, $25k/lot contributions, 5% contingency, ~$588k of total buffers) are within market range for Sydney small-lot subdivision, though civil costs have been volatile; the Gate 3 tender is the true test.

Two model-quality caveats: GST is hard-coded at 7% of GRV as a cost ($1.286M) with treatment "to be confirmed" — a swing item worth up to several hundred thousand dollars either way under margin-scheme outcomes; and land tax of $175k over two years looks approximately right but assumes only a 2-year taxable holding.

## 5. The Two Tranches — Money Partner (Fixed) vs Project Partner (Equity)

### 5.1 A waterfall ambiguity the IM leaves open

The IM's worked example gives a $500k equity investor "14% of net profit" = ~$362k (72%), treating all $3.5M of capital as pro-rata. But if (say) $1.5M of the pool is fixed-tranche capital capped at 37.5% total return, the equity pool is only ~$2.06M and the residual after the fixed coupon ($562.5k) is ~$2.02M — a $500k equity ticket would then earn ~$489k ( **~98%**), not 72%. Either the IM's example understates the equity return, or the true waterfall differs from what Section 7 describes. **The Shareholders' Deed waterfall must be read line by line before choosing a tranche** — the difference is 26 points of return on the same base case.

### 5.2 Scenario comparison — $500k ticket, 2.5-year term

| Scenario | Fixed tranche (37.5% coupon) | Equity tranche (residual waterfall) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| +5% prices | +$187.5k — covered | +$838k (+168%) |
| Base | +$187.5k — covered | +$489k (+98%) |
| −5% | +$187.5k — covered | +$270k (+54%) |
| −10% | +$187.5k — covered (thin) | +$119k (+24%) |
| −20% | **at risk — coupon not fully covered** | −$74k (−15%) |

Equity outperforms fixed in every scenario down to roughly −12% prices on time; fixed outperforms below that, and in any long-delay scenario. Note the fixed tranche's protection is priority _within an unsecured pool_ — it ranks ahead of equity investors but behind $11.1M of senior debt and, in practice, behind the trade creditors of the project entity. It is not principal-protected in any meaningful sense.

### 5.3 Probability-weighted view (illustrative weights: +5%/base/−5%/−10%/−20% at 15/40/25/13/7%)

|  | Expected return | Expected IRR |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed tranche | ~$174k (34.9%) | ~13% p.a. |
| Equity tranche | ~$404k (80.8%) | ~26% p.a. |

Interpretation, not advice: the fixed tranche is being paid roughly a private-credit coupon for what is economically subordinated, unsecured, illiquid exposure to a two-person sponsor — the risk-pricing is unattractive relative to listed private credit or first-mortgage funds paying 9–11% with actual security. The equity tranche is the only position whose upside matches the risk actually being taken; it is a concentrated development bet on (a) a 21-lot approval and (b) the sponsors executing. Whoever takes equity should size the ticket assuming a possible pro-rata capital call of ~25–30% of the original commitment in a downside.

## 6. Fee & Alignment Analysis

| Fee | Amount (incl. GST) | Issue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Acquisition | $143,000 | **Related party** (Barossa = Saxena). Payable regardless of outcome. |
| Management | $165,000 | Fixed; reasonable quantum. |
| Performance | $814,710 | 20%+GST = effective 22%; struck on profit **before** deducting the other two fees, and with **no hurdle/preferred return** to investors. |
| **Total** | **$1,122,710** | = 32% of investor capital; 30.3% of gross project profit. |

A market-standard promote for this risk would be 20% **over an 8–10% investor preferred return**, calculated after all fees. As structured, the manager earns its full promote from the first dollar of profit while investors bear a possible capital call. Negotiating a preferred-return hurdle is the single highest-value term change an incoming $500k–$2M investor could seek — and with ~$2M still unraised, there is negotiating leverage.

## 8. Conditions Precedent (before any commitment)

1. **Planning:** Written planning advice (independent town planner, not the sponsor's) confirming the MLS across the whole site and that 21 lots is achievable by DA alone under the current LEP — or the honest alternative pathway and timeline.
2. **Security:** Second-ranking registered security (or at minimum a GSA + specific charge) from Auswide Development Six in favour of Bensley Road Pty Ltd; review of the "binding agreement" by your own lawyer.
3. **Waterfall:** The executed Shareholders' Deed waterfall, resolving the Section 5.1 ambiguity; push for a preferred-return hurdle before the 20% promote.
4. **Capital call mechanics:** What happens if downside equity cannot be raised — dilution formula, forced-sale triggers, and whether existing investors can be compelled.
5. **Sponsor verification:** ASIC current & historical extracts on all four entities and both directors; bankruptcy register; AFCA/NCAT/court search; completion statements and two investor references from Developments One and Two.
6. **Valuation & debt:** The independent valuation (which the senior lender requires anyway) and a credit-approved term sheet — not "modelled at 65%."
7. **Feasibility file:** The v0.2 model itself, the SIVA DD report, DA456/21 documents, and written confirmation of the GST margin-scheme treatment and stamp duty from the project accountant.
8. **Compliance:** Confirmation of how wholesale status is being verified for all investors, and legal comfort that the fixed-return share class is not an unregistered debenture/MIS issue.

# Outlier Ads for English / Language-Learning Apps (Meta, TikTok, YouTube), 2023 to Nov 2026

Notes compiled 2026-11-21. Labels used below:
- **[VERIFIED]**: a figure stated by the company, its executives or the platform (for example a Meta case study or a founder interview).
- **[3P-DATA]**: third-party ad-intelligence scrapes (AdManage, Sprites, Airbridge, Upspring, TikTok Creative Center through AnyAPI). These are observational and can be incomplete.
- **[OBSERVATION]**: my own reading of the creative or the data, not a published figure.

A key limitation applies throughout. The Meta Ad Library does not publish spend or performance for commercial ads. "Run duration" and "number of variants" are the standard proxies the industry uses for which ads are winning. They are not proof of ROAS.

## Q1. Which specific ads or creatives have run longest or scaled hardest?

### Takeaway
The clearest documented outlier is Praktika's creator video: a coach correcting a learner's mistakes in a short conversation. It drew about 30M organic views across Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, and the CEO says it took Praktika from about $1M to $10–12M ARR in a single month. Paid teams then cloned the format across countries. In the Meta Ad Library, the longest-running language-app ads are less dramatic. Babbel's "3 Weeks To Learn A New Language... Challenge Accepted" copy has run 1,187 days, Preply's creator "guess the language" partnership videos have each run 230–388 days, and Speak's French-market English ads have run 358 days.

### Inferences
- Praktika's outlier was **organic creator content first, then paid replication**. The ads visible in the Meta library are not the breakout assets. Anyone copying should look at the creator posts, not just the ad library.
- Long run durations cluster around **simple promise copy plus a time-bound claim**: Babbel's "3 weeks", Speak's "1,000 phrases in 7 days" and "3.7× faster". They also cluster around **creator partnership videos that are entertaining on their own**, like Preply's multilingual-group quizzes.
- Variant counts in the thousands (Speak's 2,630; Preply's 2,771 for "Feel confident in any subject...") point to Advantage+/dynamic creative. The same copy is paired with many video edits, so a "winning ad" is really a winning copy-plus-format template.

### Gaps
- Verbatim hook, exact length and date of Praktika's "Gustavo" video were not found in the transcript summary. The likely period is 2023, based on the ~20× revenue growth from 2023 to 2024 cited by Amplitude, but this is unconfirmed.
- No spend estimates were retrieved. AdManage notes that spend is its own estimate, but its pages did not show spend for these brands.
- No Foreplay, Motion or Atria write-ups specific to language apps were found within the call budget.
- No YouTube Ads Leaderboard entries for language apps (2023–2026) were found.

## Q2. What hook patterns repeat across winners?

### Takeaway
The dominant 2025–2026 pattern is **"myth correction" / "never say X"**: "Never, and I mean never, say 'I'm fine, thank you'", "Don't say 'How are you?'", "DON'T SAY THIS IN SPANISH". It is localized into a dozen or more languages and almost always followed by an AI-tutor correction demo and a free trial. Close behind are **"I understand but I can't speak"** (fear of speaking), **job interview / meeting anxiety** (career), and **"guess the language"** creator quizzes (curiosity and entertainment).

### Inferences
- The "Never say 'I'm fine, thank you'" pattern works because it combines four things:
  - (a) a mild threat of embarrassment aimed at a phrase every textbook learner knows;
  - (b) instant payoff (you learn something in 5 seconds);
  - (c) a natural bridge to the product demo (the AI corrects you);
  - (d) it translates easily, because the hook is in the viewer's native language and only the English phrase stays English.
  This is the paid, scaled version of Praktika's "coach corrects mistakes" creator structure. **[OBSERVATION]**
- Asian and emerging-market ads (ELSA VN, Praktika Brazil, Learna Hindi/Thai) lean on **career and income** and **family shame**. US/EU ads (Babbel, Speak) lean on **speed, method and travel**. **[OBSERVATION]**
- Verbatim "POV: you froze in a meeting" hooks were not found. The closest equivalents are ELSA's "Can you join the meeting?" and Loora's "Prove it, tell me about yourself."

### Gaps
- No retention or CTR data was found showing which hook archetype wins head-to-head. The evidence is frequency and longevity of variants, not measured performance.
- "Native speakers don't say..." appears mainly in organic teacher-creator content. A paid ad using that exact wording was not captured.

## Q3. How did Praktika, Speak, Duolingo, Babbel and Preply achieve breakout growth? Any published numbers?

### Takeaway
- **Praktika**: one creator video turned into a replicated format, plus a career-not-English positioning in Brazil. It now reports a profitable $50–55M ARR.
- **Duolingo**: grew almost entirely through organic, character-led TikTok rather than paid ads. Follower counts are well documented (about 35–50K to 16M), and the "Death of Duo" stunt produced about 1B organic views and a record DAU quarter.
- **Speak**: scaled through Korea first, then a 2025–26 US push visible as a surge of Meta ads.
- **Babbel and Preply**: run sustained, high-volume Meta programs. Babbel leans on image ads with lifetime-deal offers. Preply leans on creator partnership ads.
- No public CPI or ROAS was found for any of these brands.

### Cited Findings

**Praktika**

## Q4. What role do AI-avatar and creator UGC ads play in 2024–2026?

### Takeaway
They are now the default format. The AI-tutor category (Praktika, Speak, Loora, Learna, Talkpal, Falou, BoldVoice) almost uniformly runs **presenter or creator video, then an on-screen AI-tutor correction demo, then a free trial**. Tutor marketplaces (Preply) run creator partnership ads at scale. Praktika's on-screen AI avatar made its content "visually understandable." The company is now building an AI-generated video pipeline for ads, but it found that "unhinged" avatars do not belong in the product.

### Cited Findings
- On Praktika's avatar in marketing, the podcast host remarked that it "makes the content marketing... much more visually understandable to see this thing talking." Turaev: "even back then... we really focused on making sure that the avatar is there." — [Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-praktika-built-a-profitable-50m-arr-ai-business-adam-turaev-praktika)
- "Unhinged avatars were going viral in upper funnel," but adding cursing avatars in-app upset users. "We're not serving AI companions, we're serving AI coaches." — [Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-praktika-built-a-profitable-50m-arr-ai-business-adam-turaev-praktika)
- Creator policy: "Do not tell influencers how to do their job." Fixed fee plus view bonus. Winners are handed to the paid team and re-shot with internal and contractor creators. — [Sub Club](https://subclub.com/episode/how-praktika-built-a-profitable-50m-arr-ai-business-adam-turaev-praktika)
- Praktika's Creative Strategist role calls for 5–7 AI-generated videos and ad creatives a week, choosing "the right model for each shot." — [LinkedIn job](https://www.linkedin.com/jobs/view/creative-strategist-at-praktika-ai-4443155012)
- Loora ads use an AI avatar in "single-scene, AI-driven" executions ("The best way to improve your speaking is to practice every day."). — [Meta Ad Library 2708334596234035](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=2708334596234035), [1581060787352516](https://www.facebook.com/ads/library/?id=1581060787352516) **[3P-DATA]**
- Preply: 511 creator partnership ads out of 7,846. Speak: 29 partnership ads. — [AdManage: Preply](https://admanage.ai/ad-library/preply), [AdManage: Speak](https://admanage.ai/ad-library/speak)
- ELSA's TikTok seeding uses teacher-creators across markets: @giorgiasenglish (Italy, 5 videos), French "85% de réduction" (85% off) sponsored posts, and Indonesian testimonials ("I'm done looking for someone who would practice english speaking with me..."). — [Airbridge](https://www.airbridge.io/en/app-revenue/elsa-speak)
- In TikTok Creative Center top ads, AI-tutor ads (Learna AI) run long (54–96s) with an "Talk to an AI Tutor, download Learna AI" CTA in Arabic. — [TikTok Creative Center Top Ads](https://ads.tiktok.com/business/creativecenter/inspiration/topads/) **[3P-DATA]**
- A practitioner view argues that "format libraries beat creator deals every time." It warns that if an ad promises a specific outcome and the install lands in generic onboarding, "the intent signal is wasted." — [GrowthCurve](https://growthcurve.co/what-the-best-language-learning-apps-get-right-about-growth) (opinion)

### Inferences
- The 2024–2026 operating model has three stages:
  1. Seed many micro-creators (fewer than 5K followers, paid a fixed fee plus a view bonus).
  2. Detect organic outliers.
  3. Re-shoot them with UGC rosters and AI-generated avatars, and localize into many languages with dynamic creative (hundreds to thousands of variants).
  **[OBSERVATION]**
- The AI avatar serves two roles: it is the product (an AI tutor) and the proof inside the ad. Showing the tutor correcting a mistake turns the hook's promise into an on-screen demonstration, the "demonstration-as-claim" mechanism in Upspring's taxonomy. **[OBSERVATION]**

### Gaps
- No controlled data was found comparing AI-avatar ads with human-creator ads (CTR, CPI, hold rate) for language apps.
- No published case studies for these brands were found on TikTok for Business or Meta, apart from ELSA's qualitative Messenger case.
